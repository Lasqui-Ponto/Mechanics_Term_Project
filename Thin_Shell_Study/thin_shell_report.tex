\begin{abstract}
A screened source is often replaced by one effective scalar charge when
predicting a fifth force. This study tests two separate steps: estimating that
asymptotic charge and using it to predict the force at a finite distance.
For $V(\phi)=\Lambda^5/\phi$ in the weak conformal-coupling limit, a two-domain
collocation solver computes \StudyPoints\ nonlinear spherical boundary-value
problems with $10^2\leq\rho_c/\rho_\infty\leq10^5$ and
$10^{-3}\leq m_\infty R\leq0.3$. An active-shell Yukawa integral predicts the
charge within 10\% at \StudyASWithin\% of this grid. A simpler point-factor
times finite-volume control reaches the same coverage; its median error is
\StudyPointMedian\%, versus \StudyASMedian\% for the radial integral.
The additional radial weighting is therefore not established as the cause of
the improvement. Even the numerical asymptotic charge underpredicts the local
force at $1.5R$ by as much as \StudyForceNearMax\%; \StudyForceNearFailures\ grid
points exceed 10\% error. The maximum errors fall to \StudyForceFiveMax\%
at $5R$ and \StudyForceTenMax\% at $10R$. At the illustrative force-ratio
threshold $\alpha_\star=\StudyThreshold$, replacing the tail-based reference
with the direct nonlinear gradient changes \StudyTailFlips\ decisions.
A refined-domain audit of \StudyAuditPoints\ selected points preserves the
direct-force decisions. These are numerical validity and decision-boundary
results for an idealized model, rather than detector sensitivities or new
experimental exclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research question and scope}
Chameleon screening changes the strength and range of a scalar-mediated force
through the ambient matter density \cite{KhouryPRL,KhouryPRD}. A dense body can
have a nearly constant interior field and a small effective scalar charge,
despite an appreciable microscopic matter coupling. The familiar long-range
thin-shell estimate provides a useful, inexpensive description of that effect.
Its use in an observation, however, involves both a source approximation and
a force-propagation approximation.

The questions here are: (i) how accurately do specified charge models reproduce
the nonlinear spherical solution, (ii) how much of the improvement comes from
finite shell volume rather than radial Yukawa weighting, and (iii) how far from
the source must a probe be before the measured asymptotic charge predicts its
local force accurately? The last question supplies a direct mechanics
connection: the acceleration of a small test body is set by the local field
gradient, not by an asymptotic coefficient alone.

Analytic thin-shell and no-shell matching, including finite interior-mass
effects, already exists \cite{TamakiTsujikawa}. Real laboratory geometries have
also been solved numerically \cite{Elder2016,Schlogel2016}. This study makes no
priority claim for these methods or for Yukawa shell integration. Its limited
contribution is a reproducible charge-to-local-force error analysis with a
controlled ablation and an explicit threshold reference. All sources and data
are available in the
\href{https://github.com/Lasqui-Ponto/Mechanics_Term_Project/tree/agent/improve-chameleon-project/Thin_Shell_Study}{public study repository}.

\section{Physical model and normalization}
Use natural units $\hbar=c=1$ and the reduced Planck mass
$\mpl=(8\pi G)^{-1/2}$. For a positive scalar field take
\begin{equation}
 V(\phi)=\frac{\Lambda^5}{\phi},\qquad A(\phi)=e^{\phi/M_c},
 \qquad M_c=\frac{\mpl}{\beta}.
\end{equation}
The static weak-gravity equation is
\begin{equation}
 \nabla^2\phi=-\frac{\Lambda^5}{\phi^2}+\frac{\rho}{M_c}e^{\phi/M_c}.
\end{equation}
To make the approximation explicit, define $x=r/R$, $u=\phi/\phi_\infty$,
$\widetilde\rho=\rho/\rho_\infty$, and $D=\phi_\infty/M_c$. The full
dimensionless equation has coefficients
\begin{equation}
 u''+\frac2x u'=-\frac{A_0}{u^2}+B_0\widetilde\rho e^{Du},
 \quad A_0=\frac{\Lambda^5R^2}{\phi_\infty^3},\quad
 B_0=\frac{\rho_\infty R^2}{M_c\phi_\infty}.
\end{equation}
At the exact ambient minimum $A_0=B_0e^D$ and
$(m_\infty R)^2=A_0(2+D)$. The present experiment deliberately takes
$D\to0$; it does not silently discard an independent coupling parameter.
In that limit
\begin{equation}
 \phi_{\min}=\left(\frac{\Lambda^5M_c}{\rho}\right)^{1/2},\qquad
 m_{\rm eff}^2=\frac{2\Lambda^5}{\phi_{\min}^3},\qquad
 \lambda_C=m_{\rm eff}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The two inputs are $\delta=\rho_c/\rho_\infty$ and $\mu=m_\infty R$:
\begin{equation}
 u''+\frac2x u'=\frac{\mu^2}{2}[\widetilde\rho(x)-u^{-2}],
 \quad \widetilde\rho=\begin{cases}\delta,&x<1,\\1,&x>1,\end{cases}
 \quad u'(0)=0,\quad u(\infty)=1.
 \label{eq:bvp}
\end{equation}
Consequently $u_c=\delta^{-1/2}$ and $m_cR=\mu\delta^{3/4}$.

\subsection{A parameter is not a measured geometric thickness}
The normalization uses the total body mass
$M_s=4\pi\rho_cR^3/3$ and $\Phi_N=GM_s/R$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \eps\equiv\frac{\phi_\infty-\phi_c}{6\beta\mpl\Phi_N}
 =\frac{2(1-\delta^{-1/2})}{\mu^2\delta}.
 \label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}
This is the potential-defined thin-shell parameter, not an exact geometric
thickness. In the relevant large-interior-mass asymptotics,
$\eps\simeq\Delta R/R+1/(m_cR)$; identifying it with $\Delta R/R$ requires
the latter correction to be negligible \cite{TamakiTsujikawa}.
For example, at $(\delta,\mu)=(10^5,0.1)$ the parameter is $1.99\times10^{-3}$
while $1/(m_cR)=1.78\times10^{-3}$. We do not label an assumed shell radius
as a thickness measured from the BVP.

\section{Charge models and the ablation control}
All charge predictions below use the same surface-referenced tail convention.
The clipped leading long-range law is
\begin{equation}
 \qlr=\min(3\eps,1),\qquad \mu\ll1.
\end{equation}
Clipping at one is already an interpolation through the transition, rather
than a controlled expansion there. Two finite-range amplitude hypotheses are
\begin{equation}
 \qsurf=\qlr,\qquad \qctr=e^{-\mu}\qlr.
\end{equation}
These are different approximations to the same coefficient. They are not a
physical ambiguity in the theory. For a fixed solution, changing from
$e^{-\mu(x-1)}/x$ to $e^{-\mu x}/x$ simply changes the coefficient by
$Q^{\rm center\ reference}=e^\mu Q^{\rm surface\ reference}$ and leaves
the field and force invariant. Nor does ``surface amplitude'' mean the exact
field of a material spherical shell. Finite-mass matching supplies more detailed
approximations than these two simple hypotheses \cite{TamakiTsujikawa}.

A massless matching model with a frozen core gives the assumed radius fraction
$q=\sqrt{\max(1-2\eps,0)}$ and finite active volume
\begin{equation}
 \qms=1-q^3.
\end{equation}
This construction is an approximation; the finite-$\mu$ core is not rematched
self-consistently. To isolate the extra radial weighting, compare
\begin{align}
 \qpv&=e^{-\mu}(1-q^3),\\
 \qas&=\frac{3e^{-\mu}}{\mu^3}[g(\mu)-g(\mu q)],
 \qquad g(z)=z\cosh z-\sinh z.
 \label{eq:ablation}
\end{align}
Both have the same assumed active volume and point Yukawa factor; only
$\qas$ includes the additional spherical radial weight. In the fully active
limit the exact linear uniform-sphere result also contains the background
subtraction $1-\delta^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm lin}=(1-\delta^{-1})\frac{3e^{-\mu}}{\mu^3}g(\mu).
\end{equation}
The shell models omit this small background term on the high-contrast grid.
It is retained in the weak-source verification.

\section{Asymptotic charge and local acceleration}
In the linear exterior region define the numerical charge by
\begin{equation}
 u=1-s\qnum\frac{e^{-\mu(x-1)}}x,\qquad s=\frac{\mu^2\delta}{6}.
\end{equation}
Two estimators, used only in a suitable tail interval, are
\begin{equation}
 Q_A=\frac{x(1-u)e^{\mu(x-1)}}s,\qquad
 Q_\nabla=\frac{x^2u'e^{\mu(x-1)}}{s(1+\mu x)}.
\end{equation}
The reported $\qnum$ is the median of $Q_A$ where the nonlinear exterior source
and its linearization differ by less than 2\%. Agreement with $Q_\nabla$
checks tail extraction; it does not establish linearity near the source.

For a small, non-backreacting probe with a supplied response factor $Q_t$, the
nonlinear reference and the Yukawa-tail prediction are respectively
\begin{align}
 \ad(x)&=\left|\frac{a_5}{a_N}\right|_{\rm direct}
      =\frac{2\beta^2Q_t}{s}x^2u'_{\rm num}(x),\label{eq:direct}\\
 \at(Q,x)&=2\beta^2Q_tQ(1+\mu x)e^{-\mu(x-1)}.
 \label{eq:tail}
\end{align}
Here $a_N=GM_s/(R^2x^2)$ is the body's Newtonian reference, not the acceleration
of an infinite uniform environment. The main force products use $\beta=Q_t=1$.
The factor $Q_t$ is not a numerical solution of a two-body screening problem.

There are two distinct multiplicative errors:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\at(Q_j,x)}{\ad(x)}
 =\frac{Q_j}{\qnum}\,\frac{\at(\qnum,x)}{\ad(x)}.
 \label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
Thus charge error and propagation error can reinforce or cancel each other.
A charge-accuracy ranking need not be a local-force-accuracy ranking.

\section{Numerical method and verification}
The density jump at $x=1$ is preserved with separate inner and outer
collocation domains mapped to a shared coordinate. The solver matches both
$u$ and $u'$ at the surface. At the finite outer boundary it imposes the
asymptotic Robin condition
$u'(x_{\max})=(\mu+1/x_{\max})[1-u(x_{\max})]$.
The charge-scaled variables $x(u-1)=sv$, or $u=\exp(sv/x)$ when positivity
is helpful, improve conditioning. Both represent the same nonlinear equation;
they are not thin-shell inputs to the BVP.
The Robin condition is asymptotic: in the logarithmic representation it is
imposed on $x\log u/s$ to the same linear-tail order. The domain audit checks
the effect of the remaining nonlinear boundary terms.

SciPy's fourth-order collocation algorithm solves a $25\times29$ logarithmic
grid over the ranges in the abstract, nominally at $x_{\max}=160$ with
tolerance $2\times10^{-4}$. Every point converges. The solution is sampled
directly at \StudyRadiusSamples\ probe radii in $1.02\leq x\leq20$, including
$1.05,1.5,2,3,5,10$. The weak-source uniform-sphere benchmark has maximum
relative charge error $\StudyWeakError$.

the reported contrasts but is not a convergence certificate for every grid
point or a continuous parameter-space bound.

\section{Interpretation, limitations, and reproduction}
The supported research result is a distinction between charge error and
finite-distance acceleration error, with a directly tested distance dependence.
The ablation also shows that the more elaborate radial integral is not required
to obtain its charge coverage on this restricted grid. Whether that changes
for $\mu\gtrsim1$, other potentials, or different source shapes remains open;
such extensions require new numerical checks and a broader comparison with
existing matched solutions.

The model is static, spherical, homogeneous in each region, nonrelativistic,
$n=1$, and conditional on $D\to0$. A real experiment additionally needs the
source's dimensional scale, chamber walls, finite test-body response, source
modulation, detector noise and systematic errors. Existing atom and levitated
force searches illustrate the importance of those ingredients
\cite{Hamilton2015,Sabulsky2019,Yin2022}. This isolated-sphere map supplies no
new physical $(\Lambda,M_c)$ exclusion and does not reassess those experiments.
For an actual forecast the relevant quantity is a predicted differential
signal divided by its uncertainty, not an arbitrary force-ratio cut.

From the repository root, run:
\begin{verbatim}
python -m Thin_Shell_Study.run_study --workers 4 --force
python -m unittest discover -v
\end{verbatim}
The first command generates both languages' figures, scan and local-force CSVs,
distance and threshold-sensitivity tables, refined audits, JSON metrics, and
the numeric TeX inputs used by both reports and decks. Cached data carry a
schema and numerical-model/settings fingerprint; a charge-only or stale cache
is recomputed. The JSON records the package versions, source fingerprint, and
Git revision at the run. The Korean-only rendering command reads the same
metrics and threshold, with no independent numerical defaults.

\section{Conclusion}
The numerical charge benchmark remains reproducible, but it is not by itself
a force benchmark near the source. The point-factor/finite-volume control
matches the active-shell integral's charge coverage, limiting the causal claim
for radial weighting. The direct-gradient comparison exposes a
\StudyForceNearMax\% worst tail-force error at $1.5R$, decreasing to
\StudyForceFiveMax\% at $5R$ and \StudyForceTenMax\% at $10R$ on this grid.
Using that local force changes \StudyTailFlips\ decisions at the selected
threshold. These results provide a concrete way to control the approximation
used between a screened source and a classical-mechanics observable.
